# Live OCO Underperformance — Investigation Resolution

_Last verified: 2026-05-14_

This document is the resolution addendum to the runtime report
`data/analysis/live_perf_gap_report.md` (gitignored; generated by
`scripts/diagnose_live_performance_gap.py`). It captures the
investigation that started from that report's headline finding — a
−7.7 pip/trade pooled underperformance vs training expectation on
2026-05-13 — and the conclusion reached on 2026-05-14.

## Question asked

The originating analysis observed that live OCO trades on 2026-05-13
lost money on average, well beyond the +6 to +8 pip mean per trade the
training-set candidates predicted. The opening hypothesis was that
live execution differed from backtest because of a missing price-based
stop-loss during the hold horizon, and the proposed fix was to add a
hard SL at barrier distance to the JForex market orders.

## Verdict

**The underperformance is not caused by a bug.** Live execution is
faithful to the strategy at every layer except bar-boundary
construction, and bar-boundary construction is sensitive to which tick
stream the system reads from. The observed gap is the natural noise
floor of running on a different tick stream (JForex broker) than the
strategy was trained against (Dukascopy). Sample size on the day in
question (13 trades) cannot distinguish that noise floor from regime
variance. No code-level fix can eliminate it without a structural
change to the bar-definition rule or the tick-source policy.

The 9 PRs landed during this investigation are infrastructure cleanups
that made the above answer empirically verifiable. None of them
changed the strategy; several closed silent failure modes that had
been masking the underlying picture.

## What was ruled out, with the evidence each rule-out rests on

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Missing price-based stop-loss in live | Ruled out | Training labels' P&L distribution is *not* barrier-capped: `gross_std_test` is 5–7× the barrier distance for the live UIDs (`tick_opportunity_mining/*_oco_candidates.csv`). Training expects uncapped losses during the hold horizon; adding an SL would *create* a training/live divergence, not fix one. |
| Slippage / execution-layer divergence | Ruled out | Per-trade live-vs-rerun comparison across 13 trades: mean slippage −0.07 pips, median 0.00 pips. `bar_deviation.csv` `max_abs_close_delta_pips ≈ 1e-12` where bars do match. |
| Tick-data quality difference | Ruled out | Live JForex ticks vs Dukascopy historical ticks for GBPUSD 2026-05-13: median bid diff 0.000 pips, mean +0.002 pips, std 0.067 pips. 99.3% of live ticks have a hist tick within 100 ms. Same market, sub-100ms timing jitter. |
| Stale threshold schedule blocking live | Ruled out | `server.py` reads `threshold_schedule` but never consults it — confirmed by grep. Live serving uses only the rolling-quantile path. The "Schedule Has Today: False" flag in the original report was a false alarm. (PR #168 removed the dead read.) |
| Silent `threshold = 2.0` fallback skewing diagnostics | Real but downstream | The diagnostic was silently substituting `threshold = 2.0` when rolling history was insufficient or when the schedule lacked today's row. 600/806 GBPUSD rows in the 2026-05-13 governance run were tagged this way without a flag. (PR #168 replaced with explicit `threshold = NaN` + `threshold_block_reason`.) Affects diagnostic legibility, not live decisions. |
| Per-symbol statistical underperformance | Not significant | At n = 1–6 trades per symbol, no individual symbol's gap is significant at α = 0.05 vs the training mean. Pooled across 19 trades, z ≈ −2.8 (p ≈ 0.005) — real but one trading day. |

## What was found instead

Live and governance build bars from different tick streams:

- **Live**: JForex broker ticks. On 2026-05-13 GBPUSD: 83,463 ticks
  in the overlap window.
- **Governance/training**: Dukascopy historical ticks. Same window:
  82,176 ticks. **Live receives 1.6% more ticks per day.**

The bar-closing rule is "every 1000 ticks". With a 1.6% tick-count
delta over a day, every 1000-tick boundary on the live side drifts
relative to its governance counterpart. Over a full session, ~30% of
bars don't pair across streams (`bar_deviation.csv`: 66 missing + 117
extra live bars out of 442). The bars that *do* coincide carry
identical prices and produce identical decisions; the bars that don't
fire at different wall-clock moments, with different feature-window
contents, and therefore produce different pred_probs. With a
threshold gate sitting at ~0.77, even a small feature shift can flip
selection on or off.

That is the entire explanation: live and governance are looking at
the same market, scored with the same model and the same threshold,
but the bar **buckets** drift apart because the rule that closes them
depends on tick count, and tick count depends on which feed you read
from.

The originating −7.7 pip/trade gap is therefore the joint effect of
(a) one bad trading day's regime variance and (b) the noise floor
introduced by bar-stream drift. Neither is actionable as a code fix
in the strategy itself.

## What remains open

These are policy / planning questions, not code defects:

- **Bar-definition rule.** Switching from tick-count bars to time-count
  bars (e.g. every 5 s) would close the same bars on any tick stream.
  Disruptive — invalidates all current training data, models, and
  governance locks.
- **Tick-source policy.** Either feed Dukascopy ticks into live, or
  replay JForex broker ticks through the governance training
  pipeline. Each has cost and contractual implications.
- **Aggregate over more trading days.** With more samples the bar-drift
  noise floor will become measurable, and threshold / sizing can be
  calibrated against it.

None of these is urgent. The system is producing the decisions its
design specifies. The decisions are slightly different from what
governance would produce on Dukascopy ticks, by a margin consistent
with the tick-source delta.

## How to re-verify this conclusion

The deviation diagnostic now produces an apples-to-apples comparison:

```
uv run python scripts/analyze_live_governance_deviation.py \
  --runtime-db <path-to-live-state.db> \
  --tick-root <path-to-dukascopy-tick-root> \
  --models-dir models/oco \
  --governance-dir configs/research/governance/oco \
  --out-dir <out-dir> \
  --lookback-days 7
```

Read the resulting `bar_deviation.csv`, `signal_deviation.csv`, and
`*_governance_predictions.parquet`. Confirm that:

1. `max_abs_close_delta_pips` is ~1e-12 on matched bars.
2. `governance_predictions` parquets carry populated thresholds (no
   `ROLLING_HISTORY_GAP`) when `audit_logs` has prior probs for the
   candidate.
3. The signal-count delta between live and governance reflects the
   bar-construction drift, not a model or threshold bug.

If any of these no longer hold, the conclusion above may need to be
revisited.
